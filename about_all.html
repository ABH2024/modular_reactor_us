What was this study about?

Please read this page only after you have finished the study.

The question

When two people interact, one person often acts first (they wave, push, or offer something) and the other person reacts. In a conversation, the person who listens is not still either: they nod, change posture, move their hands and their face, and when they speak, their gestures and face go with their own voice. Our research builds a computer program that creates the reaction of one person: the movement of their body and hands, and the expression of their face. It does not get any written description of what is happening.

Why we need your opinion

We can measure with numbers whether a created movement looks like real human movement. But numbers cannot easily tell whether the reaction actually answers the other person: whether it comes at the right moment and goes in the right direction, while listening and while speaking. People see this immediately. That is why we asked you to compare two versions side by side.

What you saw

Part A (two people). The blue person was the same in both videos; the yellow person reacted to the blue person. There was no sound.

  • Which yellow person's movement is a more appropriate response to the blue person's movement? (How the response fits the interaction, including its timing.)
  • Which yellow person moves more naturally? (Smoothness and plausible body and hand movement.)

Part B (body and hands). Left: the real partner (Speaker), with the sound of both voices. Right, top and bottom: two versions of the GREY person (Reactor), who reacts. The grey person is real in some videos, computer-made in others.

  • Which grey person reacts better to the partner, while listening and while speaking? (Nods, posture, gestures at the right moment; gestures that go with their own voice.)
  • Which grey person moves more naturally? (Body and hands: smoothness, no stiff or shaking movement.)

Part C (faces). Close-up: left the real partner's face (Speaker); right, top and bottom, two versions of the GREY face (Reactor). Both grey faces share the same head and body; only the expression differs.

  • Which face reacts better to the partner, while listening and while speaking? (Expressions and nods at the right moment; a face alive with its own voice.)
  • Which face moves more naturally? (Smooth expressions, no frozen or jerky face; the mouth follows the voice.)

What you compared

  • Some reactions were made by our program.
  • Some were made by other published programs. Some of those programs are given a written description of the action, which our program does not get.
  • Some were the real recorded movement of the person.

In each pair you did not know which was which. Which version was on the left and which on the right (in parts B and C: on top and at the bottom) changed from screen to screen and between participants, and the order of the screens was shuffled within each part for every participant.

Your data

We keep your anonymous code, your answers, the time you took, how you played the videos, your screen size and your browser name. If you want your answers removed, send your code to the researcher.
